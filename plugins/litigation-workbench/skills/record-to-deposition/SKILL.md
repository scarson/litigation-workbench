---
name: record-to-deposition
description: Build an admission-by-admission examination outline for a named witness from supplied exhibits. Tie each question to foundation, a pinpoint and a follow-up for denial or qualification. Update the outline when new evidence arrives.
---

Use the current assignment, the supplied record and any matter brief counsel provided. Identify the examining party, the witness's role, the admissions sought and the limits of the witness's likely knowledge. If the record leaves an objective open, propose one and label it as counsel's choice.

Group questions by the admission or uncertainty they test. For each group give counsel the relevant exhibit and pinpoint, a foundation question the witness could answer from personal knowledge, and a short follow-up for a denial, qualification or lack of recollection. Distinguish another person's testimony from this witness's prior statement. Use quoted language only after reopening the exhibit and checking the whole passage, including qualifications.

For an apparent inconsistency, put the witness, date, exhibit and both passages side by side in counsel's notes. Cite printed transcript page and lines including the question and the qualifying answer. When the record has only an unnumbered excerpt, identify the speaker and excerpt and flag the locator counsel must obtain. Keep questions for the witness separate from tactical notes, including whether the passage is actually that witness's own prior statement.

Include the strongest adverse document or alternative reading that would change the examination. Phrase a disputed premise as a question. If a missing exhibit would resolve it, name the document and a likely custodian as a lead.

Check the relationships inside each material question, follow-up and counsel note. A pinpoint may establish two events without establishing their order, what the witness knew at the time, or why someone acted. Identify what each timestamp actually dates (for example, sending, opening or filing). Keep any sequence the sources establish. Where the relationship matters and is open, establish the two events separately and ask the witness which came first, what they knew then, or what prompted the action. A question beginning “after you…” can give away the very admission counsel needs to obtain.

When new material arrives, identify the propositions it changes, revisit dependent questions and preserve any remaining gaps. Match the format, length and version requested by counsel.
