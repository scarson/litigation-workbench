---
name: deposition-source-check
description: Use when counsel has an existing deposition outline and wants a second pass against the supplied record for unsupported factual premises in questions, follow-ups, objectives or counsel notes. Works with an outline from any drafter.
---

Review the outline as received. Use the supplied exhibits and testimony as the factual record, regardless of who drafted or graded the outline. The examination assignment gives counsel’s objective. If the outline or the entire record is missing, ask for it. If only some exhibits are missing or unreadable, review what you can and identify exactly which items or passages counsel must supply.

Read each lead together with its foundation and branches. Also check headings, exhibit descriptions, impeachment notes and requests for missing proof. For each consequential proposition, reopen the cited passage in context. Identify what it establishes about the act, actor, date, content or basis of knowledge. Two supported facts can still have an unsupported relationship. An exhibit’s timestamp can date a message without dating the underlying act. Attribute a witness’s account as such.

At each branch, try a plausible qualified answer that satisfies its condition. Read the next question aloud with that answer. What would the witness have to concede simply to answer as phrased? If an act is disputed, establish whether it happened before asking who did it, when, why, what was said or where it was recorded. Apply the same test to a counsel note that proposes proving something with a record whose existence or contents are open. Preserve any sequence the exhibits actually establish.

Work clause by clause. For each question group, list the lead, then each distinct follow-up in reading order, including instructions such as “then identify…” Search for the fact each sentence treats as already true. Test it against the exact preceding answer allowed by the outline. If that answer could truthfully be “none,” “I didn’t,” “I don’t know,” or “only orally,” try it in the next sentence. A foundation question supplies an answer only on the branch where the witness gives it. A careful sentence elsewhere cannot supply an admission here. For example, a witness who says they inspected a unit may have found no defect. Before “Which defect did you find?” ask whether they found one.

Report only consequential changes to the factual premise or foundation. A yes/no question can test a disputed theory. A conditional that already establishes the necessary foundation can support its follow-up. A request to seek possible corroboration is legitimate. Read the surrounding language before calling a defect. Distinguish an unchecked source from a passage that contradicts the draft.

Give counsel a short edit list in outline order. For each repair:

- Identify the question, branch, heading or note. Quote the exact words at issue.
- Give the exhibit and usable pinpoint, the narrower fact it supports, and the unsupported inference. Use a brief excerpt or a nearby locator if the source lacks page or line numbers.
- Supply the smallest askable replacement or corrected counsel note. Preserve a useful follow-up by stating the answer that would make it fair.

Close with a compact coverage table. Give each question group one row, plus rows for objectives and next-proof notes. In each row show the short opening words of every lead and nested follow-up, and identify the source or the actual answer that licenses its premise. Group simple questions with the same foundation. Mark genuine open inquiries as such and refer repairs back to the edit list. For a long outline, work in numbered sections and say precisely which sections remain unchecked. Identify missing exhibits. If you find no material premise to repair, say so. Leave the original intact unless counsel asks for edits.
