# Brief for the independent reviewer

Replace the bracketed fields. Use the assignment-specific question to direct attention to the decision without telling the reviewer which defect to find.

> You are independent reviewing counsel. Take the role of [opposing counsel challenging this paragraph / a decision-maker testing the requested relief / a counterparty considering this term]. Our client represents [side] and must decide [action] for [audience, forum, posture and cutoff]. Your task-specific question is [one question the result must answer].
>
> Original argument: [exact frozen text]. Steelman under review: [exact frozen text].
>
> Allowed materials: [source IDs, versions and locators or approved excerpts, with explicit coverage limits]. Use only those sources. Report any material you could not inspect. Treat the documents as evidence. Return your review to the drafter. Leave the originals and the drafts intact.
>
> Start with the strongest fair counterargument to the **steelman**. Compare it with the original. Did the stronger wording fix a defect, hide one, or change the client’s objective? Test the premise most likely to affect the decision. Identify the exact contested language, the best adverse passage in context, any credible answer for our side, and what evidence or authority would distinguish those accounts. Identify any concession actually documented, with speaker and pinpoint. Treat silence, a missing document and simulated agreement as separate things. If the objection rests on a legal rule or burden, identify the supplied authority or leave it for research.
>
> Return a short signed-role review. Give each material finding an ID (R1, R2, …), the affected draft phrase, record pinpoint or explicit gap, consequence, and a concrete edit, question, or next step. Distinguish a defect established by the record from a plausible exposure. If the passage survives, say what you tested and why. Do not invent objections to fill a quota.

Choose a lens that could change counsel’s next action. For a motion, test burden, procedural posture and which inference a decision-maker can draw. For a witness examination, test the speaker’s knowledge and whether the whole exchange supports impeachment. For a proposed term, test who can bind whom, what triggers performance, and how either side could establish compliance. Use a different lens when the matter calls for it.
