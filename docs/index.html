What can we prove? Proof matrix

Bring the document you rely on and the best one for the other side.

We represent [side] and need to decide [question]. Build a one-page, editable proof matrix from the attached record. Give each material proposition an ID, the best evidence on each side with exhibit and pinpoint, and the witness or document that could resolve the gap. Distinguish what someone observed from what they inferred. Use the evidence-matrix skill if available.

Check the row that could change your decision. Open both passages, including the lines around them.

What should I ask this witness? Deposition outline

Bring the witness’s role, the admission you want and the exhibit you would put in front of them.

Prepare a two-page examination outline for [witness and role] about [objective]. For each admission, give me a question I can ask, its foundation, the exhibit and pinpoint, and a follow-up for a denial or qualification. Keep my notes separate. Include the hardest document for our side. Use the record-to-deposition skill if available.

Check one proposed impeachment against the printed transcript. Whose words are they, and does the rest of the answer qualify them?

How much can we support? Damages workbook

Bring the demand, ledger or invoices, and the contract provision that makes payment due.

As of [date], reconcile the demand to the ledger and the amount this record supports as due. Make an editable workbook with visible formulas, source IDs and a tie-out. Show each disputed trigger or offset separately. Identify the missing document that could move the number most. Use the damages-reconciliation skill if available.

Check one invoice from contract to ledger to due date. Change a disputed assumption in a copy and see which total moves.

Where does this draft overreach? Brief audit

Bring a versioned draft and the primary authority or exhibit behind a consequential paragraph.

Audit [draft paragraph] against the attached authorities and record. Break it into claims. For each material defect, identify the source and pinpoint, what the passage supports in context, and the narrowest defensible rewrite. Separate citation identity, proposition support and current treatment. Leave the original intact. Use the brief-audit skill if available.

Check the proposed rewrite against the majority opinion or exhibit. For a second read, give LegalQuants Litigation the same sentence and primary source in a fresh chat.

Fictional example · Can the buyer still inspect on Friday?

Original. “The seller agreed to extend the contractual inspection deadline through Friday.”

Steelman. “Before the Wednesday cutoff, the buyer put its request for Friday in writing, and the seller’s project manager said Friday worked for scheduling. The exchange gives the buyer a concrete basis to insist that Friday remains available for inspection.”

Independent objection. A fresh Astra reviewer at xhigh flagged two points. R1: An open dock only establishes access. R2: The buyer asked the project manager a question, while the contract directed notices to a designated inbox.

Revision for the call. Adopt R1 and ask the seller to confirm continued inspection rights in writing. Adopt R2 as a notice objection and check whether a separate notice reached the inbox by the deadline. Leave the legal effect of actual receipt open.

Read the exact assignment, unedited review and revised paragraph.

How to spend the hour together
  1. First 10 minutes. Pick the decision, the sources and what a usable result would look like. Write down how long the job usually takes.
  2. Next 30. Have the lawyer drive. Produce one editable file, open it in its normal app, and trace the most consequential claim to its source. Give one correction in the lawyer’s own words.
  3. Last 20. Add one exhibit or change one assumption. Compare the versions. Save the reviewed file and the instruction worth reusing. Choose a second matter where the same method would save time.

To reuse his own method, ask: “Turn my two corrections into a short instruction for the next matter. Strip out all names, figures and conclusions.” Save the instruction separately and test it on a different example. The LegalQuants Companion can also help shape an assignment or debrief the session.
