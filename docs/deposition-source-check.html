What the blind second pass caught

The released skill fully caught five of eight previously identified bad premises across seven outlines. It identified one more in its coverage table but left counsel’s objective unchanged. It missed two. The plain request fully caught two of eight. The initial skill fully caught one. Requiring the reviewer to account for each nested question improved the result in this replay.

Measured by outline, the released skill supplied every expected repair in four of the seven, identified the problem without supplying an edit in one, and missed the recorded defect in two. Full credit required the actual passage, a correct source locator and a usable correction. A different valid edit earned no credit toward the target.

Three edits worth looking at

  • A follow-up worth keeping. “Who made the entry decision?” came after a witness described sponsorship. The program desk might still have the application pending. The reviewer kept the useful question and added its missing condition: “Was a decision made on East’s entry?” Only then, “Who made it?” Credit review, outline 1B.
  • A caveat stranded in the notes. The reviewer correctly said the freight messages could not date the carrier’s first binding commitment. Counsel’s objective still said “Establish approval before carrier commitment.” The plain review supplied the usable edit: establish approval before the recorded booking notice and confirmation. Freight reviews, outline 1B.
  • Two misses to check yourself. “Its audio” assumes a recording has sound. The recording practice says nothing about the channels captured. “What part did you personally play in supplying the information” assumes the pitch owner supplied the CRM loss reason. The exhibit established ownership and the team’s usual process. Ask whether the witness contributed anything. AV evidence · CRM evidence.

What a flag is worth

The revised pass proposed additional edits on all seven previously cleared outlines. Its AI adjudicators accepted 24 as material and rated five optional. Those are judgments about the same records, not 24 independently confirmed errors. For example, the first source audit treated “obtain its speaker, date, terms, and supporting record” as a discovery instruction. The new review treated it as assuming a document exists. I would keep the instruction for counsel and ask the witness whether any writing exists. On the credit outline, the new adjudicator rejected the objection to asking a witness what record might refresh their memory. Freight outline 2C · Credit outline 1B.

On the separate print-job example, the revised pass caught both planted errors: the press log did not date the restart relative to completion, and knowing the approval rule did not establish that someone actually approved closure. It left the deliberately grounded outline intact, including the order the ticket timestamps did establish. Both print-job reviews.

Did the two misses justify changing the skill?

We kept version 1.4.0. We tested the released skill against one extra paragraph asking the reviewer to find the exact evidence linking a person to an act or an item to a feature. Eight fresh reviews used two new fictional disputes. Each record had two nearly identical outlines. Changing just a run or worksheet number made the same assertion supported in one outline and open in the other.

  • Furnace archive. Both reviewers caught the assumption that a retained controller file contained a clip-temperature series for run K-17. Both preserved the same comparison for K-19, whose inventory verified the series. Each also suggested looking at K-19 while reviewing K-17. That optional detour would not establish what happened on K-17. Record and outlines.
  • Repair allowance. Both challenged the assumption that the witness chose a percentage on worksheet R-64 because she initialed its arithmetic check. Both preserved the identical follow-up on R-61, where her signed note said she chose the percentage. Record and outlines.

Each method caught both unsupported targets with a pinpoint and an actionable repair. Neither requested an edit to either supported outline. Each offered one optional change on an outline with a defect. The candidate put its optional switch of furnace runs ahead of the repair that kept counsel’s original objective. The frozen rule required improvement in both families to change the skill.

The different industries and identifiers tested whether the reviewers could use the evidence beyond the earlier audio and CRM examples. Both records contained explicit support for one item alongside a gap for the other, and each method ran only once on each outline. Harder records may still produce the original misses. This comparison did not measure attorney editing time. Median returns were about 463 words for the released skill and 441 for the candidate.

How to inspect the test

Two graders had awarded 10/10 to fourteen outlines in our four-record study. A later source audit identified seven that needed repairs. We kept all fourteen, including the seven previously cleared. One retail outline had two distinct premises to check. Before running any new reviews we froze the skill, the eight targets and what would count as a full catch. Read the protocol and target key · Pre-test commit.

Each outline first went into two fresh Codex chats using GPT-6.1 Sol at high effort. The chats received the same lawyer request, one unbranded outline and its packet. Only one had the initial skill selected. Neither saw the scores, reference findings or other answers. We retained the first completed review. For each pair, a separate Astra chat saw randomly assigned letters and judged the wording against the exhibits and target key before the letters were decoded.

After seeing missed branches, we revised the skill to show a coverage table for each question group. We froze that version, repeated the same fourteen reviews in fresh chats, and added a separate fictional case with two versions of its outline. An Astra chat checked each new review against its exhibits and expected findings without receiving its method label. This second run used the same scoring criteria.
