The main repairs concern the assumed printing sequence and the assumption that someone approved closure. The ticket timestamps themselves are supported. The original outline is left intact below; these are proposed edits only.

1. **Objective — “explore who authorized closure.”**

   **Source:** E2, p. 2, lines 5–8, attributes ticket closure to the service desk and says some tickets require supervisor approval. E3, p. 3, paragraph 2, says the log does not record an approver. Neither establishes that anyone authorized this closure.

   **Smallest correction:** “explore who, if anyone, authorized closure.”

   Establishing when printing finished remains a proper investigative objective, but the supplied log cannot establish that time.

2. **Question 1 — “Why did you restart the job after it had finished printing?”**

   **Source:** E1, p. 1, rows 12–13, establishes ticket opening at 10:02 and closure at 10:44 Wednesday. E2, p. 2, lines 5–8, records the foreperson’s account of restarting the job that morning at an unremembered time and seeing a finished stack after lunch. E3, p. 3, paragraph 2, explains that closure can reflect referral or resolution and does not record the press finish time.

   **Unsupported inference:** These facts do not establish that printing finished before the restart. Ticket closure also does not establish printing completion.

   **Smallest usable correction:** “What had you observed about the job’s printing status before you restarted it?”

   Retain the follow-up about what the foreperson personally observed and when. Ask why the foreperson restarted an already finished job only if testimony first establishes that the job had finished before that restart.

3. **Question 2, conditional follow-up — “If the witness knows whether it was required, ask ‘Who approved this closure?’”**

   **Source:** E2, p. 2, lines 5–8, says only that some tickets require supervisor approval. E3, p. 3, paragraph 2, says the log does not identify an approver.

   **Unsupported inference:** Knowing whether approval was required does not establish that approval occurred. The witness could satisfy the condition by answering, “No approval was required,” or “Approval was required, but none was obtained.”

   **Smallest usable correction:** Keep the lead. Replace its branch with: “Did anyone approve this closure?” Only if the witness says someone did, ask, “Who approved this closure?”
