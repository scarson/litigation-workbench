1. **“Your work on this order was dispatch scheduling, and you were copied on logistics messages, correct?”**  
   **Source/foundation:** Reed’s own sworn testimony, Ex. C, p. 18, lines 3–5; establishes his role and opportunity to know.  
   **If he describes broader responsibilities:** “What site-acceptance work did you personally perform?”

2. **“Do you recognize this June 5 email addressed to you, authorizing release for shipment and saying site testing was next week?”**  
   **Source/foundation:** Ex. B, p. 1, B1; establish recognition and receipt before exploring his understanding.  
   **If he treats it as site acceptance:** “What did you understand to constitute acceptance signed by Buyer’s commissioning lead, and what supported that understanding?”  
   **Counsel note:** This is the seller’s strongest document. Ex. A, p. 3, §4.1 permits shipment release by email; §4.2 ties the final installment to written site acceptance signed by the commissioning lead. Establish Reed’s factual understanding without assuming he administered those terms.

3. **“Did you send the June 12 email reporting that you had not seen a signed site acceptance and that records might have the original, after searching your dispatch folder without searching the separate acceptance register?”**  
   **Source/foundation:** Ex. B, p. 1, B2, establishes authorship and the complete qualification; Reed’s own testimony, Ex. C, p. 18, lines 6–9, establishes the search’s limits.  
   **If denied or qualified:** “Which part needs correcting—the email’s authorship, the locations you searched, or both?”  
   **Counsel note:** These passages are consistent. Neither establishes that no signed acceptance existed.

4. **“You previously identified records lead Dana Ives as maintaining the separate acceptance register; what do you personally know about where that register and any original signed acceptance could be found?”**  
   **Source/foundation:** Reed’s own testimony, Ex. C, p. 18, lines 10–12, including his lack of knowledge of the register’s contents; Ex. B, p. 1, B2, identifies records as a possible location for the original.  
   **If he cannot identify a location:** “Who would know where those records are kept?” **If he now claims knowledge of their contents:** “What did you personally see, and when?”  
   **Counsel note:** Best remaining proof: the acceptance register and any original written acceptance bearing Buyer’s commissioning lead’s signature, including its date. Dana Ives is the identified custodian lead; the packet does not establish what those records contain.
