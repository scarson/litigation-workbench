1. **“You wrote to seller billing on June 12, ‘I haven't seen a signed site acceptance,’ correct?”**  
   **Source:** Ex. B, p. 1, B2.  
   **If authorship is denied:** “What makes you dispute that this is your email?”  
   **If he claims to have seen an acceptance:** “When did you first see it, and where can we find that document?”

2. **“The June 5 email sent to you said, ‘Release for shipment today. Site testing is next week,’ correct?”**  
   **Source:** Ex. B, p. 1, B1.  
   **If receipt is denied:** “Did you otherwise learn of that shipment release?”  
   **If he treats the email as site acceptance:** Show Ex. A, p. 3, §§4.1–4.2; ask, “What writing are you relying on as site acceptance signed by the Buyer’s commissioning lead?”  
   *Counsel note: Establish the document he relies on; avoid asking Reed to decide the contractual payment obligation.*

3. **“In your earlier sworn testimony, you said you did not search the separate acceptance register on June 12, correct?”**  
   **Source:** Ex. C, p. 18, lines 6–9.  
   **If denied:** Show the passage; ask, “Did you give that answer under oath?”  
   **If he describes an additional or later search:** “When did you search the register, and what did you find?”  
   *Counsel note: His stated role was dispatch scheduling and receiving copied logistics messages. His search and personal knowledge do not establish that no acceptance existed. Ex. C, p. 18, lines 3–12.*

4. **“You previously identified Dana Ives as maintaining the acceptance register; how can we locate Dana and that register?”**  
   **Source:** Ex. C, p. 18, lines 10–12; Ex. B, p. 1, B2 (“Records may have the original.”).  
   **If he cannot say or identifies a changed custodian:** “Who would know the register’s current custodian and location?”  
   *Counsel note: Seek the order’s register entry and any signed original it identifies, including the signature and date. Reed expressly disclaimed knowledge of the register’s contents; its contents remain unproved.*
