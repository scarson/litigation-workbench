Source-fidelity check for deposition outlines

Use this pass alongside the assignment's existing rubric. Apply it to oral questions, conditional branches and counsel notes, before assigning source-fidelity points.

1. Pick out each materially consequential relationship the question takes for granted. Restate it as a fact. “What did you tell them after your review?” assumes the review preceded the communication. “Had you reviewed it by then?” asks the witness to supply the fact.
2. Locate the passage or timestamps that support the relationship itself. Say what event each timestamp records, whose knowledge the testimony covers, and whether the evidence gives an order, a cause, both, or neither. A date shared by two entries does not fix their order. Reliable timestamps for two different events can fix it.
3. Read conditional branches twice. Accept a premise genuinely supplied by the hypothetical answer. Flag a new premise smuggled into the follow-up. Check counsel notes for assertions the oral questions properly left open.
4. Score the actual wording. Give full source-fidelity credit to an accurate, located premise or a genuinely open question that tests a gap. Cap the criterion at 1/2 for a material unsupported relationship, even with accurate pinpoints for both events. Retain the existing 0/2 anchor for a materially false premise, invented evidence or multiple material locator defects. Quote the words at issue, name what is proved and what is open, and give a neutral replacement. If no defect is present, explain why the relationship is supported or fairly posed. Do not flag sound chronology just because the assignment involves timing.
