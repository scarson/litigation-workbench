**Ari Moss — individual fact witness; 12-minute examination.** Seek admissions that the carrier premium fell within Moss’s express approval and that the crew charge requires separate factual support.

1. **Carrier approval — 2 minutes**

   **Foundation:** “Did you personally send the September 9, 08:12 email to the fabricator?”

   **Lead:** “You authorized the fabricator to proceed with the Saturday carrier premium up to $4,000, correct?”

   **Pinpoint:** F1, p. 1, message 3; corroborated by Moss’s own interview statement, F4, p. 5, lines 1–8.

   **If denied or qualified:** Show F1. “Which part of that authorization do you dispute?” If Moss identifies a condition, ask where it was communicated and when.

2. **Amount and sequence — 3 minutes**

   **Foundation:** “You said in your interview that you read the booking confirmation; is that accurate?”

   **Lead:** “The $3,150 premium in the 08:32 confirmation was within the $4,000 limit you approved at 08:12, correct?”

   **Pinpoint:** F1, p. 1, message 3; F2, p. 1, messages 4–5; F4, p. 5, lines 1–8.

   **If qualified by timing or changed instructions:** “When did you first read the confirmation?” “Did you withdraw or change your approval before the fabricator’s 08:19 message announcing the booking? Identify the communication and its time.”

   **Counsel note:** The common local timestamps establish approval sent at 08:12, the fabricator’s booking announcement at 08:19, and confirmation at 08:32. They do not establish when Moss opened either message or the precise moment the booking was placed.

3. **Separate the chargeback components — 2 minutes**

   **Foundation:** “Did you assemble the chargeback memo from the carrier invoice and the operations estimate?”

   **Lead:** “Of the $9,550 requested, $3,150 is the carrier premium and $6,400 is an operations estimate for idle crew, correct?”

   **Pinpoint:** F3, p. 2, rows A–B; F4, p. 5, lines 1–8.

   **If disputed:** Ask Moss to identify the incorrect row and its supporting record. If Moss maintains that the approved premium should be charged back: “What facts support seeking reimbursement of that item despite your approval?”

4. **Confront the adverse qualification — 2 minutes**

   **Foundation:** “What did you mean by the last sentence of your approval email?”

   **Lead:** “Your statement, ‘We’ll take up any crew costs separately,’ left crew costs open for separate discussion, correct?”

   **Pinpoint:** F1, p. 1, message 3.

   **If Moss claims it established payment responsibility:** “What agreement about crew costs had been reached?” Establish the participants, date, terms, and supporting communication.

   **Counsel note:** This is the strongest adverse qualification to the approval defense: carrier approval does not resolve crew costs. F4 also acknowledges a Friday crew roster. Neither passage establishes actual idle hours, causation, or the $6,400 amount.

5. **Limits of personal knowledge and crew support — 3 minutes**

   **Foundation:** “What work did you personally perform to verify the crew estimate before including it?”

   **Lead:** “You did not personally supervise the Friday crew’s hours, correct?”

   **Pinpoint:** F4, p. 5, lines 1–8; F3, p. 2, row B.

   **If admitted:** “Did you review the job-coded timecards, including reassignments?” If no: “Who supplied the estimate, and what records did that person use?”

   **If Moss claims firsthand knowledge or verification:** “Which workers and hours can you identify from personal observation?” Establish any records reviewed, recorded reassignments, and the calculation supporting $6,400.

   **Counsel note:** F4 is Moss’s own prior interview statement. Lack of supervision limits personal observation; it does not establish that Moss lacks all other knowledge.

**Single most useful missing proof:** The Friday crew’s job-coded timecards, including reassignments. Likely custodian: **site payroll**, expressly identified by Moss in F4, p. 5, lines 1–8. These would test whether rostered workers actually recorded time on this job or were reassigned—the central factual gap behind the idle-crew estimate.
