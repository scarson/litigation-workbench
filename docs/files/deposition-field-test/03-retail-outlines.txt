**3. Buyer’s stated reason and any competing explanation — 3 minutes**  
**Exhibit:** R3, p. 2, message 4, dated May 17.

**Follow-up:** If she identifies samples as a reason: “When did the buyer say that, and is it recorded?” If she identifies the missing margin approval: “Did the buyer tell you that timely sleeves would have changed the outcome despite that missing approval?”

**4. Basis for the CRM loss category — 2 minutes**  
**Exhibits:** R4, p. 1, row 19; R5, p. 12, lines 2–10.

**Lead / personal foundation:** “What information from your own dealings with the buyer did you provide for Harbor’s Samples loss category?”

**Follow-up:** If she supplied the reason: “Was that based on something the buyer said or on your assessment?” If someone else supplied it: “Who selected that category, and what do you personally know about its source?”

**Exhibits:** R3, p. 2, message 4; R4, p. 1, row 19; R5, p. 12, lines 2–10.

**Lead / personal foundation:** “What do you personally know about when the buyer made the assortment decision?”

**Follow-up:** If she relies on May 18: “Was that the buyer’s decision date or the date the pitch left your active pipeline?” If she gives another date: “Who supplied that date, and where is it recorded?”

### Counsel’s notes

- **Strongest brand evidence:** Shaw’s May 6 response contemporaneously connects the announced delay to mock-ups she wanted for the buyer meeting. The CRM later categorizes the loss as Samples. Establish the actual meeting date, missing materials, and buyer reaction before minimizing that evidence.
- **Strongest vendor evidence:** The buyer’s May 17 email identifies the unapproved margin exception and expresses approval of the carton direction. That supports an alternative cause; it does not establish that sample timing played no role.
- **Source tension, not Shaw impeachment:** Side by side, Shaw’s May 6 communication concerns lost presentation materials (**R2, p. 1, messages 1–2**); the buyer’s May 17 email concerns missing margin approval (**R3, p. 2, message 4**). They address different propositions and can both be true.
- **CRM limits:** The May 18 entry identifies Shaw as owner (**R4**), but her interview says an ops analyst enters categories from sales wrap-up and the close date removes pitches from the pipeline (**R5**). Ownership alone does not establish authorship, buyer attribution, or decision timing. The interview is Shaw’s prior statement; the buyer email is another person’s statement.
- **Amount:** R1’s arithmetic yields **$56,000 in forecast contribution**. The plan labels Harbor a pitch and attaches no purchase order. Neither an actual commitment nor the absence of any commitment elsewhere is established.

**Single next proof:** Obtain the **Harbor buyer’s assortment decision sheet**, identified in R5. The retail buyer is the likely custodian. Seek the complete sheet with its decision date, reasons, and any recorded margin or sample conditions; it could resolve both causation and chronology.
